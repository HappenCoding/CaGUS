1. Overview & Experimental Setup

Physical ambient light transport principles and lab configuration

Section 1
Figure 1: Top view of experimental layout, occluder geometry, and camera Field of View (FOV).

Physical Sensing Principle: Motion of occluded human subjects or objects within the non-line-of-sight region periodically modulates indirect light transport paths from ambient illumination, casting subtle secondary diffuse reflections onto visible boundary surfaces that remain imperceptible to the naked eye.

Experimental Configuration: Experiments are conducted around a conference table measuring 4.85 m × 3.45 m. Human subjects traverse clockwise (CW) or counter-clockwise (CCW) along the perimeter and pause at four distinct corners (L1–L4). The system employs a single passive, fixed-FOV industrial camera pointed at a blank wall, achieving continuous NLOS tracking without active optical or RF emitters.

Physical Mechanism & Sensitivity Interpretation: The normalized polar radius in each radar chart quantifies the relative contribution of each physical sub-factor to the SNR spread, defined as max(SNR) − min(SNR). In Transport Geometry, propagation distance is the primary attenuation factor (1.000); in Illumination, spatial source distribution critically dictates shadow contrast clarity (1.000); for Materials, a normalized sensitivity radius of 0.750 demonstrates that low-reflectance diffuse surfaces still maintain sufficient transport contrast for robust non-line-of-sight tracking.

Transport Geometry

Max = Distance
  • • Distance (Spatial Span): 1.000 (Dominant Decay)
  • • Layout (Occlusion Structure): 0.386 (Geometric Constraint)
  • • Angular (Motion Angle): 0.281 (FOV Span)

Distance governs primary path loss, while favorable occlusion structures preserve high-SNR transport pathways and expand the continuous tracking boundary.

Illumination

Max = Distribution
  • • Distribution (Spatial Layout): 1.000 (Shadow Contrast)
  • • Intensity (Irradiance): 0.662 (Contrast Gain)
  • • Source Count (Multi-source): 0.334 (Diffuse Multipath)

Source distribution determines shadow boundary sharpness and contrast. Higher intensity extends sensing range, though excessive brightness risks ambient saturation.

Materials & 3D Manifolds

Radius = 0.75
  • • Materials (Sensitivity): 0.750 (Broad Robustness)
  • • Lower Manifold (Activity): a ≤ 0.24 + 0.20 × Tlow
  • • Upper Manifold (Tracking): a ≥ 0.76 − 0.20 × Tup

Even low-reflectance matte materials sustain adequate modulation response; the dual-manifold envelope delimits the high-fidelity operational regime.
